# Event and penalty cards (turn decks)

Status: requested by the owner during the build, from the Obsidian note `Cards.md`. Built as Task 13 of the expansion plan. The owner's notes are quoted; every call I made is marked.

## What the owner asked for

- Two decks, **event cards** (good, reward players) and **penalty cards** (bad, punish players), about 50 cards each, in the design of the hidden circumstance cards.
- Both activate immediately.
- Each turn, before the player buys tiles: a **coin flip** decides which deck is drawn, and the card is drawn.
- The decks are visible on screen. A flipped card is shown like the hidden circumstance cards.
- Cards can affect money, tiles, points or other players, and can reward or punish several players at once.
- The owner's example cards (Improved Circulation, Government funding, Bank Bonus, Roads or greenery, Tree planting campaign, Luxury housing, Illegal Tree Recovery, Environmental Fine, Community Complaints, "give each player 1g or lose a road network") are all in the decks.
- Each deck has its own **back**, so you can see at a glance whether it is an event or a penalty.

## How it plays

1. A planner's turn starts (after the hand-off card for people). A coin flips: heads draws an event card, tails a penalty card (my call: a fair coin).
2. The card is dealt face down from its deck, turns over, and its effect happens. A card with a choice shows its options (keys 1 and 2, or click); computer planners pick on their own.
3. The planner then buys their tiles as usual.
4. Each card is drawn at most once per game (50 + 50 cards; at most 32 are drawn in a 4-player game).

## Rules every card follows (my calls, written so they are the same everywhere)

- Gold never drops below zero. A player cannot pay more than they have.
- "The player with the most or least ..." means everyone who ties; if all players tie, all of them count.
- A card that changes prices or the number of tiles you may buy lasts **for this turn only**.
- Free tiles go to a stock and are placed with a **Free tile** button (key B), like the free roads. They cost nothing and are not purchases. They carry over to later turns, and are lost if still unplaced after the last turn (the game warns first).
- Points won or lost through cards are a separate line in the final scoring ("Turn cards").
- A choice option you cannot afford (for example "pay 3 gold" with 2 gold) is shown but disabled; at least one option is always available.
- Losing a tile removes a random matching tile from the mat.
- "Luxury housing" gives **1 point per large apartment once** (up to 3), not a permanent extra citizen, to keep scoring simple. This is the one place where I did not follow the owner's example word for word.

## What the cards can do

Gold (for you, for everyone, for the poorest, taken from the richest, per tile you own, if a condition holds), points (the same ways), free tiles and free roads, cheaper or dearer tiles for a turn, one more or one fewer purchase, losing tiles or roads or free roads, cancelling a loan, paying other players, moving gold from the richest to the poorest, and choices between two options. Conditions are things like "your roads connect every home", "no loans", "pollution 7 or less", "you own no school".

## Look

The cards use the same collectible-card face as the hidden circumstance cards (frame, ribbon, picture window, parchment rule box, corner gem, sheen). Events are green with a star and a green gem; penalties are red with a warning sign and a red gem. The backs differ: event decks have a golden star burst, penalty decks have hazard stripes and a warning triangle. 40 pictures (20 per deck) are shared by the 100 cards (my call: 100 unique pictures were not feasible; every card has its own title, rule and story).

On the game screen the two decks sit under the construction site as piles of card backs with the number of cards left. The turn's card stays on the player banner as a chip you can hover to read again.
