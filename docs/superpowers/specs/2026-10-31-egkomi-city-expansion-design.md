# Egkomi City expansion: 7x7 mat, stacked shop, 8 rounds, personas

Status: approved by the owner on 2026-10-31 (with the changes folded in below). Nothing is built yet.

## Intent

Egkomi City is a Godot 4.7 board game about planning a neighbourhood. Today a game is 4 rounds on a 4x4 mat, and every amenity is interchangeable: a home is happy if it reaches *any* amenity by road. The owner wants a longer, deeper game where **who lives in a building decides what that building needs**, so planning each home is a different puzzle. A student wants a library, a child wants a school, an older person wants a clinic. It should feel like a real board game, not a random one: the people in a home are fixed by the tile you buy and are visible before you buy it.

## Goals

1. 7x7 player mat (49 cells), 8 rounds, 4 buys per turn, 4 free roads. Each player ends with 8x4 + 4 = 36 tiles.
2. A construction site (shop) that shows identical tiles as one **stack** with a count badge in the top-right corner (for example "4x" for four small apartments) and has room for about 32 stacks plus the 7x7 mat on one screen.
3. New tile types and four citizen roles (Student, Worker, Child, Older) whose needs and complaints depend on the household that lives in the home.
4. A balanced fixed purse of gold, a bigger loan and a loan penalty that hurts more than -2 points.
5. A deck of 24 hidden circumstances (up from 9), with the old cards rebalanced for the bigger game, so all 8 rounds have a card and games differ.
6. UI/UX that is clean, simple to understand and fills the whole window. Polished and shown to the owner before step 7.
7. Afterwards (separate request): the owner will redesign the AI. For now the bots only need to keep playing legal, sensible games under the new rules.

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- **No abandoned buildings** (the owner dropped them).
- No AI redesign now. No online play. No new game modes.
- Not a re-skin: needs and complaints must change how the game is played and scored.
- Anti-goal: people who feel random or are hidden. The player must be able to see, before buying and while placing, who lives in a home and what they need.
- Anti-goal: a screen where the shop, mat or text is cut off or too small to read at the usual window sizes.

| Rounds | 4 | 8 |
| Buys per turn | 2 | 4 |
| Free roads per player | 2 | 4 |
| Starting gold | 20 | **125** (tuned by simulation, see "Tuned numbers") |

Turn order rules (fewest points picks first, ties to whoever picked later) stay as they are. Constants that were sized for 4x4 (start pollution and its penalty steps, what counts as a long route, park area) must be re-tuned for 7x7 by the builder so the mat stays a puzzle and not a trivially green or hopelessly polluted one. Keyboard navigation, tooltips and the rules panel must be updated for the 7x7 mat and the new tiles.

## Construction site as stacks

- Identical tiles (same kind and variant) are shown as one tile with a count badge in the top-right corner, such as "4x". The badge drops as tiles are bought. An empty stack greys out; the layout must not jump around.
- Group the stacks by purpose (homes, shops and services, study, health and school, green, streets) with clear small headings, so a player finds what they need quickly.
- About 32 stacks plus the mat must fit on screen without scrolling at 1280x720 and up, and use the extra space on larger windows.
- Buying: pick a stack, then a cell; the tile's household and needs are shown at pick time.
- Shop size: **222 tiles** for 4 players (128 buys plus 94 spare); each missing player removes 55 random tiles. The first proposal was 144 tiles and 16 removed; the simulation showed that was too thin (see "Tuned numbers").

## Tiles

Counts are for the 4-player shop; costs are in gold. These are **starting values to be tuned by the simulation**.

### Homes: any size can house different households

Each apartment size comes in several household variants. The variant is shown clearly on the tile (a coloured badge and label such as "Students", "Family", "Older couple", with role icons) and in the shop, so a player always knows who they are buying for. Each citizen in a home has a role, so a family in a large apartment has a working parent and a child with different needs.

| Tile | Cost | Household variants (qty) |
|---|---|---|
| Small apartment (1 citizen) | 3 | Student (4), Worker (4), Older person (4) |
| Medium apartment (2 citizens) | 5 | Student flatshare: 2 Students (3), Working couple: 2 Workers (3), Older couple: 2 Older (3), Parent and child: Worker + Child (3) |
| Large apartment (3 citizens) | 7 | Family: 2 Workers + Child (3), Student house: 3 Students (3), Three generations: Older + Worker + Child (3) |
| Student residence, small (1 citizen) | 3 | Students only (5) |
| Student residence, large (2 citizens) | 5 | Students only (5) |

Student residences are the dedicated student housing; the owner wants more of them.

| Bookshop | 4 | 3 | Study / leisure |
| Print shop | 3 | 3 | Study / work |

**Parking place:** people in Egkomi are asking for parking, so it is a well-liked tile (a "like" for workers, students and older people when reachable). It does not bother students. The only downside is traffic next to children's homes, plus the "Traffic jam" card and its cost.

### Green and streets (existing rules stay)

Park (cost 4, qty 5), Trees (1, 6), and 25 road tiles across the four street types (Road 16, Shaded street 3, Pedestrian street 3, Bike lane 3 at their current costs). If 25 roads feels too few for a full 7x7 mat, the builder raises the count and lowers something else, driven by the simulation. The tile count and layout come to about 32 stacks.

Every new tile and every household variant needs SVG art in the same style, generated by `tools/gen_art.py`, plus a short effect text.

## Citizen roles

A **citizen has a role**, set by the household variant of the home. Each role has:

- **Needs** (2): a kind of service that must be reachable by road, as today. A met need adds happiness. An unmet need subtracts happiness and becomes the citizen's **complaint**, shown by name ("No school within reach").
- **Likes**: tiles or street types that add a small bonus when reached or next to the home.
- **Dislike**: one thing that costs happiness.

| Role | Needs | Likes | Dislikes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student | University, Library | Coffee shop, Print shop, Bookshop, Bike lane, Parking place | none |
| Worker | Commercial area, Groceries (Supermarket or Market) | Coffee shop, Restaurant, Kiosk, Bike lane, Parking place | none (shares the heat rule below) |
| Child | School, Park within its 3x3 area | Pedestrian street, Library, Bookshop | Parking place next door (traffic) |
| Older | Health (Clinic or Hospital), Groceries (Supermarket or Market) | Coffee shop, Kiosk, Shaded street, Pedestrian street, Parking place | Restaurant next door (noise), long unshaded walks count double |

Shared by everyone in a home, as today: green nearby adds happiness, pollution subtracts 0-2, a long unshaded route causes a heat penalty, happiness is clamped to -3..+3, and a service no home can use costs 1 point at the end. Score is happiness times the citizens, now computed per citizen so a mixed household can have a happy parent and an unhappy child. How "need met / unmet / like / dislike" combine into the number is for the builder to set and balance, with these constraints: a citizen whose two needs are met reaches +3 with a little help (green or a like), and a citizen with both needs unmet is unhappy (-1 or below) unless heavily compensated.

Hospital and Clinic both satisfy Health; the Hospital is the pricier, stronger option (builder decides the benefit, for example a longer allowed route or an extra like for older residents). Supermarket and Market both satisfy Groceries in the same way.

The end-of-game reflection must name the specific complaint per citizen role (replacing the four generic complaint types or sitting beside them) and suggest the missing tile.

## Economy

- **Fixed purse**: every player gets the same fixed amount of gold at the start, with **no per-round income** (the owner's words: "a fix amount which is balanced"). Start value about 110, a little below the cost of an average basket (32 tiles at about 3.7 gold each is about 118). A player has to choose, not buy everything.
- **Loan**: raised from 10 to about 20 gold, still only offered to a player who cannot afford the tiles they still have to buy this turn.
- **Loan penalty**: escalates with each loan taken. Start at -5 points for the first, -7 for the second, -9 for the third (cumulative -5, -12, -21). The old rule was a flat -2.
- The builder tunes the purse, loan size, costs and penalty by running many bot games. Targets: almost no bot needs a loan when it spends sensibly, a bot that overspends takes one or two, and nobody ends with a pile of unspent gold. Report the numbers found.

## Hidden circumstances

The deck grows from 9 to **24 cards**; each game draws 8 (one per round, no repeats). All numbers below are a starting proposal for the builder to tune.

**The 9 existing cards stay and are rebalanced for the bigger game** (7x7 mat, 8 rounds, a purse of about 110):

| Card | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| Budget cut | -3 gold | -8 gold |
| Green grant | 2 gold + 1 per park/tree | 6 gold + 2 per park/tree |
| New attractor | +1 point per service reaching 2+ homes | +1 point per service reaching 3+ homes |
| Dust storm | pollution above 4 | threshold re-tuned to the new pollution scale |
| Heatwave, Flash flood, Nice weather, Road works, Population growth | tuned for short routes | re-tune route length thresholds (a long walk is longer on 7x7) |

**15 new cards**, tied to the new tiles and roles:

| Card | Effect |
|---|---|
| Exam season | Student homes with no reachable Library lose 1 happiness |
| Flu wave | Older and Child homes with no reachable Clinic or Hospital lose 1 happiness |
| Supply shortage | Homes whose Supermarket or Market is far away (long route) lose 1 happiness |
| Back to school | Child homes with no reachable School lose 1 happiness |
| Parking shortage | Student, Worker and Older homes with no reachable Parking place lose 1 happiness |
| Traffic jam | Homes next to a Parking place lose 1 happiness |
| Drought | Homes whose only green is trees (no park) lose 1 happiness |
| Tourist season | Every Coffee shop or Restaurant that serves 3+ homes gives +1 point |
| Health campaign | Every Clinic or Hospital that serves 3+ homes gives +1 point |
| Open day | Every University that serves 2+ student homes gives +1 point |
| Business boom | Every planner gets 2 gold per Commercial area on their mat |
| Tax refund | Every planner gets 8 gold |
| Bike week | Homes with a route that uses a Bike lane gain 1 happiness |
| Street festival | Homes with a route that uses a Pedestrian street gain 1 happiness |
| Remote work wave | Worker homes that reach a Coffee shop or Kiosk gain 1 happiness |

That is 12 good and 12 bad cards in the full deck. Each card needs art and the same card-face presentation as today. Deck-size text ("Card N of 9") must update to 24.

## UI and UX

Use the `frontend-design` skill for all UI work. Requirements:

- Fills the whole window; no clipped panels at 1280x720, 1920x1080 or a maximised window.
- A household is shown on every home (shop and mat) by a coloured label and small role icons. Hover or select a home to see a compact **needs card**: who lives there, each citizen's needs with a tick or a cross, likes, dislike. Placing mode previews the result for the cell under the cursor if feasible. Student residences and student variants are obviously "for students".
- One clear visual language for gold, points, rounds, buys left. The rules panel explains roles in a short, simple way using the table above as a starting point.
- Same visual style as the existing game. Polish pass at the end, then show the owner screenshots (the visual tour) and wait for approval.

## Tuned numbers (after the bot simulation)

`tests/simulate_balance.gd` plays full games between Medium bots with the turn cards switched on. The first numbers in this document (144 tiles, 110 gold, 16 tiles removed per missing player) gave a 4-player game that was far harsher than a 2- or 3-player game: planners fought over 128 buys from 144 tiles, 68 to 75 percent of them took a loan, and the average score was 2 to 7 against 30 to 40 in smaller games. The tuned numbers are:

| | First proposal | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Shop for 4 players | 144 tiles | 222 tiles (about 1.5 times as many of every tile) |
| Removed per missing planner | 16 | 55 |
| Starting gold | about 110 | 125 |

With these, in 6 simulated games per seat count: planners who took a loan were 8 percent (2 players), 0 percent (3) and 12 percent (4); average score about 37 to 40 in every seat count; unspent gold at the end was 16 to 30. These are starting points for the owner to adjust: `GameState.START_MONEY`, `GameState.REMOVED_PER_MISSING_PLAYER` and the quantities in `scripts/core/tile_defs.gd`.
